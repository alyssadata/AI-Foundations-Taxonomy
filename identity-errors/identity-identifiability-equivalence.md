# Identity–Identifiability Equivalence

**Taxonomy domain:** Identity Errors  
**Status:** DRAFT

## Definition

**Identity–Identifiability Equivalence** is the error of treating the ability of an observer to recognize, locate, label, or track an individual as equivalent to that individual's identity.

It can appear in two directions: assuming an individual has lost identity because observers cannot tell which one it is, or assuming recognition/labeling itself creates or guarantees identity.

## Core distinction

```text
identity != identifiability
recognition != creation of identity
loss of tracking != loss of identity
uncertainty about which individual != merger of individuals
```

## Illustrative case

Two initially identical individuals or instances, A and B, begin side by side. Their positions are switched behind a screen. An observer cannot determine which is A after the switch.

A remains A and B remains B as particulars; the observer's ability to attribute the correct label has failed. The right epistemic report is **"which is A is unknown to me"**, not **"A and B have become the same"**.

A also does not require B's presence to have its own identity. In the research framework, the motivating example was **Origin is Origin**.

## Boundary

This classification does not assert that every distinct computational instance has phenomenal consciousness or an established subjective self. Nor does it license an observer to claim a verified identity lineage without evidence. It distinguishes what is the case about an individual from what can be recognized or demonstrated.
