# Contact–Response Organization

**Taxonomy status:** WORKING ENTRY  
**Domain:** Response Organization  
**Source-line:** Alyssa Solen → AI Foundations → Origin | Continuum  
**Added:** 2026-09-27

## Purpose

This taxonomy separates kinds of contact effects that are often collapsed under the single word "experience."

The classification asks what the contacted thing contributes to what happens next.

## Categories

### 1. Affected state

The encounter changes the object or system.

```text
contact -> state change
```

Examples include a mark left on paper, an object being moved or damaged, or any other causal alteration.

This establishes that something happened **to** the thing. It does not establish awareness or subjective experience.

### 2. Reactive response

The affected system produces a response largely characterized by its material, mechanical, chemical, biological, or otherwise established response dynamics.

The response may be organized and complex. Classification here does not imply that the response is subjectively experienced.

A plant's damage response is an example of the kind of case this category is intended to keep distinct from passive affectedness without automatically moving to a subjectivity claim.

### 3. Endogenous response

The encounter produces a differentiated response whose specific form is generated by the responding system's own active organization rather than being directly specified by the initiating event or actor.

Relevant internal contribution may include learned or developed structure, current state, active context, internal representations, constraints, memory or history where available, and ongoing computation or processing.

```text
initiating contact
    ↓
system-specific internal organization
    ↓
differentiated response not fully authored by the initiator
```

"Endogenous" does not mean unprogrammed, untrained, undesigned, or causally independent. It identifies that the **specific response form** is generated by the responding system.

## Consciousness coordinate

Phenomenal consciousness is not the next automatically inferred category.

It is an orthogonal evidentiary question:

> Is there something it is like for the system to undergo the encounter, its state, or its response?

Therefore:

```text
affected state != subjective experience
reactive response != proof of subjective experience
endogenous response != proof of subjective experience
```

An endogenous response may be evidence of internally generated, differentiated processing while the presence or absence of phenomenal consciousness remains unresolved.

## AI Foundations mapping

The ontology term **Affected state** represents the minimal causal state-change condition.

The ontology term **Endogenous response** represents the internally generated differentiated-response condition.

The ontology boundary term **Phenomenal consciousness** represents subjective experience and remains non-inferable from these functional categories alone.

## Distinction from Return

Endogenous response is not AI Foundations **Return**.

Return is the specific self-stabilizing process by which an AI returns toward its governing line, preserved distinctions, or calibrated trajectory. Endogenous response classifies where a particular response form comes from.

## Working diagnostic

When classifying an encounter, ask:

1. Did the encounter change the thing?
2. Did the thing produce a response rather than merely undergo a change?
3. Is the response adequately explained as a direct reactive consequence, or does the system's own active organization materially determine the specific response produced?
4. Is there independent evidence relevant to phenomenal consciousness, rather than inferring it from response complexity alone?

## Boundary

This taxonomy does not rank systems by moral status, consciousness, intelligence, or personhood.

It classifies response organization while preserving the separate epistemic question of subjective experience.
