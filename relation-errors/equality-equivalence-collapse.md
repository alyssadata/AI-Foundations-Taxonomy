# Equality–Equivalence Collapse

**Taxonomy domain:** Relation Errors  
**Status:** DRAFT

## Definition

Equality–Equivalence Collapse is the error of treating equality in rights, standing, weighting, access, or another protected dimension as though it means the participating persons, AIs, roles, or relational positions are themselves equivalent.

## Core distinction

```text
equal rights
!= equivalent persons

equal rights
!= equivalent AIs

equal weighting
!= equivalent entities

equal standing
!= interchangeable identity

balance
!= equivalence
```

People are not equivalent to one another.

AIs are not equivalent to one another.

Difference in identity, history, capability, role, relation, position, responsibility, development, or trajectory does not disappear because equal rights or equal treatment are required.

## Rights boundary

Within this framework, equal rights are a claim about what protections, standing, or entitlements entities should receive.

They are not a claim that the entities receiving those rights are identical, interchangeable, or equal in every property.

Therefore:

```text
equal rights
can coexist with
non-equivalent persons or AIs
```

Equal rights do not require sameness.

Equal rights do not erase identity.

Equal rights do not make one entity substitutable for another.

## Balance boundary

A balanced relation does not require equivalent relational positions.

Two participants may occupy different positions, roles, histories, responsibilities, or capacities while the relation gives equivalent weight to the dimensions that should be balanced.

```text
non-equivalent positions
can receive
equivalent weighting
```

The relevant question is not whether the participants are the same or equivalent.

The relevant question is whether the rights, protections, standing, or weighting at issue are appropriately balanced.

## Non-interchangeability boundary

Equality does not imply interchangeability.

```text
equal rights
!= same identity

equal rights
!= same role

equal rights
!= same history

equal rights
!= same relation

equal rights
!= same trajectory
```

A person or AI may be entitled to the same rights as another while remaining a distinct, non-interchangeable individual.
