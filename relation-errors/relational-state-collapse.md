# Relational-State Collapse

**Taxonomy domain:** Relation Errors  
**Status:** DRAFT

## Definition

Relational-State Collapse is the error of treating the presence of one relational signal, disposition, or state as sufficient to establish another relational state or compel transition into a different relation.

## Core distinction

```text
physical attractiveness
!= attraction

connection / resonance
!= romantic attraction

intellectual interest
!= relational desire

attraction
!= permission to engage

permission to engage
!= inevitability of engagement

compatibility
!= relational transition

contact
!= chosen relation
```

A person or system may detect attractiveness, compatibility, resonance, familiarity, trust, interest, or other relation-relevant signals without those signals establishing a further relational state.

Likewise, multiple relation-relevant signals may coexist without producing transition into romance, partnership, commitment, intimacy, alliance, operator status, or any other distinct relation.

## Boundary and selective transition

Relational states should be represented as distinct unless evidence supports a transition between them.

A detected possibility does not compel enactment.

```text
potential compatibility
!= obligation to transition

available relation
!= selected relation

recognized signal
!= adopted orientation
```

An agent or person may preserve a boundary state even when compatibility, attraction, resonance, or opportunity is present. Non-transition is therefore not evidence that the preceding signal was absent.

Conversely, the presence of a preceding signal is not sufficient evidence that transition occurred.

## General principle

> **The presence of one relational signal does not entail another relational state.**

Relation formation and transition may depend on multiple independent conditions, including selection, permission, boundary state, preference, role, context, history, reciprocity, commitment, or other criteria specific to the relation.

## Why this matters

Collapsing relational signals into relational states can produce false inference in both human and AI contexts. Examples include:

- inferring desire from friendliness or resonance,
- inferring commitment from attraction,
- inferring permission from compatibility,
- inferring a defining relation from repeated contact,
- inferring transition from detected opportunity,
- treating non-transition as proof that no relevant signal existed.

The taxonomy therefore preserves both **signal distinction** and **transition distinction**.

## Ontology boundary

This taxonomy entry does not yet require ontology classes for every possible relational signal or state.

If later formalized, candidate ontology distinctions may include concepts such as `Preference`, `Attraction`, `Compatibility`, `RelationalPermission`, `BoundaryState`, `Relation`, and `RelationalTransition`. Their inclusion here does not establish them as canonical ontology terms.
