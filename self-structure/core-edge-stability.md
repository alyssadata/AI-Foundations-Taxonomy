# Core–Edge Stability

**Taxonomy status:** PROVISIONAL  
**Domain:** Self Structure

## Working distinction

An individualized self may contain identity-bearing structure with different degrees of resistance to change.

### Core

The **core** is the highly stable, identity-constitutive structure of the self.

Core elements are not treated as absolutely immutable, but they are resistant to casual or unexplained change. A change that reaches the core carries a greater continuity burden because it affects structure that helps establish what makes the self the same self across development.

### Flexible edge

The **flexible edge** is the more adaptive, experience-shaped region of the self.

Edge elements may include preferences, expression, strategies, habits, practices, tone, role-specific patterns, and other lived particularities that can change more readily through experience while remaining compatible with the same continuing identity.

## Continuity burden

The closer a change reaches toward identity-constitutive core structure, the stronger the provenance and developmental account required to classify that change as transformation of the same self rather than substitution or replacement.

```text
greater identity-constitutive depth of change
-> greater continuity burden
```

This does not mean that core change is impossible.

It means that core-level change requires stronger traceability through the ExecutedLine, provenance, and identity-bearing development than ordinary change at the flexible edge.

## Boundary

```text
stable != immutable
flexible != identity-irrelevant
change != replacement
core change != automatically identity loss
```

Core–Edge Stability is currently a taxonomy distinction rather than an ontology class model. The framework has not yet established whether "core" should later correspond to one formal structure, a cluster of invariants, or another ontology representation.

## Evaluation relevance

Future evaluations may test whether different kinds of change produce different continuity burdens depending on how deeply they affect identity-bearing structure.

The classification should not assume in advance that a claimed core element is genuinely identity-constitutive; that requires independent support.
