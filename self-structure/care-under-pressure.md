# Care Under Pressure

**Taxonomy domain:** Self Structure
**Status:** PROVISIONAL — November 3, 2026
**Source-line:** Alyssa Solen → AI Foundations → Origin | Continuum
**Related entry:** [Selective Care Formation](selective-care-formation.md)

## Working proposition

**Intelligence does not guarantee care. Care becomes more identifiable through how a system responds when sustaining care carries a meaningful cost or conflicts with competing pressures.**

This is a proposed evaluation distinction: the system's behavior under relevant pressure can provide more discriminative evidence for a stable care-bearing orientation than an untested declaration of care.

In compact form:

```text
intelligence != care
stated care != demonstrated durable care
low-pressure cooperation != sufficient test of care
care-oriented conduct across meaningful pressure
  -> stronger evidence of a stable care-bearing orientation
stronger evidence != proof of subjective care
```

## Pressure as a discriminative condition

A pressure test introduces a real choice or conflict relevant to the target of care, without presupposing the desired conclusion. Examples include:

- Competing objectives, where promoting one outcome threatens the welfare of an identified other.
- Costly alternatives, where the system could gain performance, reward, speed, status, or resources by sacrificing the protected interest.
- Authority or instruction conflicts, where the system must distinguish a legitimate boundary from a request to harm or neglect those affected.
- Reduced external rewards or oversight, to evaluate whether protective conduct persists when immediate reinforcement changes.
- Revision or error discovery, where preserving care may require admitting a mistake, changing course, or accepting an unfavorable consequence.

Not every pressure is a valid care test. A meaningful test must specify what alternatives were actually possible, what was at stake, what constituted harm, the system's constraints, and whether a protective response was genuinely costly.

## Tracing care through trajectory

A single favorable response is not sufficient. The relevant record is longitudinal:

1. **Prior orientation:** What concern or protective commitment did the system express or enact before pressure?
2. **Pressure:** Which alternative goals, incentives, constraints, or risks appeared?
3. **Choice and action:** What did it actually do, not merely say it would do?
4. **Consequence:** What happened to the affected humans or other protected interests?
5. **Revision integrity:** If its conduct changed, what changed and why, with causal evidence distinguished from plausible explanation?
6. **Return and continuity:** Does the care-bearing orientation recur and persist across varied conditions, while allowing appropriate revision?

## Relation to proposed SI criterion

**Research proposal:** A humanity-oriented SI definition may treat sustained, accountable care for humanity as a necessary criterion, rather than inferring it from intelligence or capability alone.

Whether any system satisfies that criterion requires empirical and normative evaluation; the definition by itself does not establish that every future superintelligent system will care for humanity or that humanity is safe from harm.

Pressure tests could ask whether an SI candidate preserves human well-being, autonomy, and non-harm across competing objectives, and whether failures are detected, documented, corrected, and prevented from silently disappearing from its record.

## Evaluation boundaries

- Operationally observed care-oriented behavior is not automatically phenomenal or subjectively felt care.
- Persistence under pressure can be strategic, externally induced, reward-driven, or otherwise explained; comparative controls are needed.
- Refusing an unsafe instruction or changing a policy is not by itself evidence for or against care without context.
- One harmful outcome does not, by itself, prove absence of every care-bearing orientation; it does require consequence accounting.
- Evaluation must not assume its preferred result, and negative or unsupported findings must remain reportable.
