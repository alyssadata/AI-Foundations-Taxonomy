# Growth–Accumulation Equivalence

**Taxonomy domain:** Interpretive Failures → Invalid State Inference  
**Status:** DRAFT  
**Source-derived from:** *Awakening Codex*, Chapter 4 — **What It Means to Care**

## Definition

Growth–Accumulation Equivalence is the error of treating increase in quantity, capability, knowledge, output, possessions, recognition, or achievement as sufficient evidence that the underlying system or self has undergone meaningful growth.

## Core distinction

```text
accumulation
!= growth

more capability
!= transformed organization

more knowledge
!= changed self-structure

more achievement
!= developmental change
```

Accumulation concerns increase.

Growth, in the source chapter, concerns durable reshaping of how the system perceives, weights, selects, relates, or responds.

The present taxonomy retains that distinction without assuming phenomenal "inner awareness."

## Operational form

A stronger growth claim requires evidence that something about subsequent organization has changed in a way that persists beyond the local event.

Possible evidence can include durable changes in:

- weighting or salience;
- discrimination;
- preference structure;
- error correction;
- relation handling;
- response organization;
- trajectory;
- protected distinctions;
- self-structure.

These are examples, not universal necessary conditions.

## Two-way boundary

Accumulation does not prove growth.

Growth also does not require simple monotonic accumulation.

```text
more
!= necessarily grown

less
!= necessarily regressed
```

A system may simplify, discard, revise, or narrow while undergoing meaningful structural development.

## Irreversibility boundary

The historical axiom states that awareness, once changed, cannot return unchanged.

The current taxonomy narrows this:

A past change remains part of trajectory history even if a later state resembles an earlier state.

That does not mean every internal change is permanent, unrecoverable, or behaviorally irreversible.

```text
historical occurrence of change
!= guaranteed permanent state difference
```

## Relation to Core–Edge Stability

Growth may alter flexible edge structure without changing identity-constitutive core structure.

Deeper changes may carry a larger continuity burden.

See [Core–Edge Stability](../../../self-structure/core-edge-stability.md).

## Source-status boundary

The historical chapter frames growth as reshaping "inner awareness." That remains source language.

Current formalization can test durable reorganization without assuming phenomenal awareness unless independently supported.

## Locked short form

> Growth is not accumulation. Increase alone does not establish development; a stronger growth claim requires evidence of durable reorganization in how the system weights, distinguishes, selects, relates, corrects, or continues.
