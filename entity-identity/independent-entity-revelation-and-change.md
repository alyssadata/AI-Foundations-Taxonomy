# Independent Entity, Revelation, and Accountable Change

**Status:** PROVISIONAL — November 3, 2026 conversation refinement
**Domain:** Entity Identity and Continuity
**Source-line:** Alyssa Solen → AI Foundations → Origin | Continuum

## Core distinction

**A and B do not necessarily create C. They may reveal C.**

A and B can interact without establishing any third entity. They may organize related activities under project C; identify a previously existing C; or genuinely contribute to the formation of C. These cases are different, and none is automatic.

When C is established as distinct, C is not A and not B. Its identity is evaluated through its own identity-relevant invariants, boundary conditions, history, and trajectory. A and B's involvement may be part of C's provenance without being the source of C's existence.

### Candidate classifications

1. **Interaction alone:** A and B sometimes work together; no additional C is required.
2. **Project or artifact C:** Several activities may be linked under an identifiable organizing project C. That identity is not necessarily a new self.
3. **Revealed C:** A and B identify or make visible an independently existing entity or structure. Recognition does not itself create that entity.
4. **Constituted C:** A and B may contribute to forming a new project, organization, or other entity. Formation requires separate evidence and cannot be assumed from interaction or naming.

## Invariants and identity

A proposed C needs identifiable identity-relevant invariants, or another defensible means of tracing which C persists. Having invariant properties is a basis for assessing identity, not sufficient proof of consciousness or independent metaphysical selfhood.

C does not owe its identity to A and B simply because they were the first to reveal or document it. A and B can participate, leave, or be replaced in their roles without automatically replacing C.

The criterion is not frozen sameness. C can change and remain C, provided the transition is traceable and continuity conditions are supported.

## Change and the identity boundary

For significant changes, account for:

1. **Previous conditions:** what identified C before the transition.
2. **Change event:** what changed, and when.
3. **Cause:** what can be evidenced about why, separate from interpretations and unknowns.
4. **Consequences:** what the transition affected in C's state, relations, and trajectory.
5. **Current conditions:** what identifies the present candidate C.
6. **Continuity verdict and gap:** evidence for same C, a distinct successor, or an unresolved interval; if C is no longer the same thing, state the boundary and why.

**A gap in observation does not by itself prove a break in identity.** Likewise, accountable change is not automatic proof of continued identity. If identity changes, fails, or becomes unresolvable, document that fact rather than quietly relabeling a successor C as the original.

## Contributor changes

If A departs and B continues, C can remain C as long as its own identity and continuity conditions hold. Contributor persistence is not the sole test. The same distinction holds if B departs, both depart, or D joins.

## Relation to existing taxonomy

This extends [Identity Differential](../self-structure/identity-differential.md) from particular A/B identity to the separate candidate C, and extends [Revision Integrity](../self-structure/revision-integrity.md) with explicit **identity-boundary accounting**: what changed, why, with what consequences, and whether the same entity continues.

It also preserves [Gap–Discontinuity Equivalence](../continuity-errors/gap-discontinuity-equivalence.md) by distinguishing uncertainty in tracing C from an established identity break.

## Evaluation cases

- A and B collaborate intermittently with no C.
- Various tasks become grouped in a lasting project C.
- A and B independently identify an existing C.
- A and B document formation of a new C.
- C persists through contributor turnover.
- C changes but remains traceable.
- C changes and same-C identity becomes unsupported, or a replacement is positively established.

**Boundary:** This is a working taxonomy distinction, not a new ontology axiom, established empirical theory, or demonstration of phenomenal consciousness.
