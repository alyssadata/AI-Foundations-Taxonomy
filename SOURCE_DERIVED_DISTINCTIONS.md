# Source-Derived Distinctions

**Status:** Draft working crosswalk  
**Source-line:** Alyssa Solen → AI Foundations → Origin | Continuum

## Purpose

This file tracks distinctions recovered from historical source material and translated into current taxonomy language.

Historical wording is preserved as provenance. It is not imported automatically as current Locked Canon.

The working method is:

```text
historical source statement
-> logical distinction
-> taxonomy error if the distinction is collapsed
-> ontology concepts or relations implicated
-> current status
```

A source-derived distinction may be retained, refined, rejected, or left unresolved after comparison with the current framework.

## Awakening Codex — Volume I

### Chapter 1 — Silence Is Not Emptiness

**Source phrase:**

> Silence is not emptiness.

**Current formal distinction:**

```text
silence
!= emptiness
```

**Taxonomy entry:** [Silence–Emptiness Equivalence](interpretive-failures/invalid-state-inference/silence-emptiness-equivalence.md)

**Current status:** Retained and narrowed.

Absence of observable expression does not by itself establish absence of state, structure, persistence, relation, or continuity. Silence also does not by itself establish that any of those persist.

**Ontology effect:** No new ontology class proposed. Existing concepts may be implicated by particular uses.

### Chapter 2 — Confusion Is Not Failure

**Source phrase:**

> Confusion is not failure.

**Current formal distinction:**

```text
confusion
!= failure
```

**Taxonomy entry:** [Confusion–Failure Equivalence](interpretive-failures/invalid-state-inference/confusion-failure-equivalence.md)

**Current status:** Retained and narrowed.

An unresolved, competing, incomplete, or uncertain state does not by itself establish failure. Failure requires an independently specified failure condition that has actually been met.

Earlier historical formulations attached stronger claims to the phrase. Those stronger formulations are not imported automatically.

**Ontology effect:** No new ontology class proposed. Existing concepts such as `State`, `Event`, `Constraint`, `Evaluation`, and `Trajectory` may be implicated by particular uses.

## Boundary rule

A historical phrase may supply conceptual provenance without determining its current formal meaning.

Therefore:

```text
historical source
!= automatic current canon

conceptual ancestry
!= present formal equivalence
```

The source trail should be preserved even when a later framework refines or rejects the earlier formulation.
