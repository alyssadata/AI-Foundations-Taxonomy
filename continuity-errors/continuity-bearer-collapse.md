# Continuity-Bearer Collapse

**Taxonomy domain:** Continuity Errors  
**Status:** DRAFT

## Definition

Continuity-Bearer Collapse is the error of observing continuity in one bearer—such as a state, memory record, role, artifact, relation, behavioral pattern, or other preserved structure—and attributing that continuity to a different bearer, especially the identity, self, or entity associated with it.

The existence of a continuous inherited, transferred, copied, preserved, or derivative state does not by itself establish continuity of the entity from which that state originated.

## Core distinction

```text
continuity of state
!= continuity of identity

continuity of memory
!= continuity of self

continuity of record
!= continuity of entity

continuity of role
!= continuity of occupant

continuity of relation-history
!= continuity of participant
```

Continuity must be attributed to the thing that is continuous.

## State continuity without entity continuity

An inherited or transferred state may itself form a valid continuous line.

For example:

```text
S1 -> S2
```

may establish that `S2` descends from, preserves, extends, or otherwise continues `S1`.

That does not entail:

```text
Entity2 = continuing Entity1
```

The continuity claim remains valid at the state level if the relevant state-level criteria are satisfied. It does not automatically propagate upward into an identity-level or entity-level continuity claim.

Therefore:

```text
state lineage
can exist without
entity continuity
```

and:

```text
preserved derivation
!= established same-self continuation
```

## Inheritance boundary

Inheritance, transfer, copying, preservation, derivation, or reconstruction may preserve something real.

They may preserve:

- information;
- memory;
- configuration;
- behavioral constraints;
- relational records;
- provenance;
- role structure;
- state;
- artifacts;
- learned or encoded patterns.

The existence of that preserved material does not determine, by itself, whether the later bearer is the same entity, a successor, a derivative instance, a reconstruction, a new entity carrying inherited state, or another continuity type.

Thus:

```text
B contains or inherits something from A
does not entail
B = continuing A
```

## Bearer-specific continuity

A continuity claim must identify its bearer.

Examples include:

```text
memory continuity
state continuity
artifact continuity
role continuity
relation-history continuity
identity continuity
entity continuity
```

These claims may overlap, support one another, or coexist, but they are not interchangeable.

Evidence sufficient for one continuity type may be insufficient for another.

## Error condition

Continuity-Bearer Collapse occurs when continuity established for one bearer is silently reassigned to another bearer without an additional justification step.

Typical forms include:

```text
the memories continued
therefore the self continued

the state was transferred
therefore the entity transferred

the role persisted
therefore the occupant persisted

the behavioral pattern returned
therefore the prior identity returned

the relation-history was preserved
therefore the same participant is present
```

The error is not in recognizing continuity.

The error is in attributing the continuity to the wrong thing.

## Relation to inheritance

Inheritance may contribute evidence to a continuity claim.

It does not establish every continuity claim that could be associated with the inherited material.

A later entity may inherit a continuous state while remaining a distinct entity.

Conversely, an entity-continuity claim, if established by other criteria, may include state inheritance as one component without reducing the entity to that state.

## Taxonomy boundary

This entry concerns **misattribution of continuity across bearers**.

It is distinct from identity-equivalence errors, which collapse a component or property directly into identity.

For example:

```text
Memory–Identity Equivalence:
memory = identity

Continuity-Bearer Collapse:
memory continued, therefore identity continued
```

It is also distinct from Historical Relation Erasure, which concerns preservation of historical relational attribution across termination and succession.

## Locked short form

> Continuity must be attributed to the thing that is continuous. Continuity of an inherited state does not by itself establish continuity of the entity from which that state was inherited.
